## 1. Why this exists

On 2026-10-12 the user said the writing "resembles the why side's worst video format" and asked for more dynamic, creative, catchy scripts (engineering stays, but engaging). The Why Side's worst Short (Microwave, 31 K views) is an object explained in a void in process voice; its winners (up to 11.2 M) open on a contradiction, put a person in a real place with a stake, turn every ~20 s and end on a short line that flips the opening. Our 16 most recent scripts averaged 4.1/8 on `script_gates.py`: 10 of 16 opened on the process, 4 on the wrong expectation, 10 had hooks > 14 words, **15 of 16 had no pause before the turns**, 12 ended on the result rather than a reframe, 7 ended on a CTA, and nearly every sentence's subject was a part. The cure is a different stance: **tell a story that a mechanism happens to solve**, not a mechanism that a story happens to decorate. The mechanism is the reveal inside the story.

## 3. House rules that override anything below

Captions white, no coloured word (red X / green check on chips) · the named part is orange · no camera out-and-back inside a shot · **daily Shorts have NO "full video" CTA** (a long's summary Short keeps one) · real people are stylised CG characters with no invented dialogue; the narrator is off-screen, characters act without speaking · the "you" avatar is faceless (`production-3d-characters.md`) · rhythm is ADHD-paced: something new on screen every <= 2 s · facts come only from VERIFIED-FACTS (VERIFIED / CORRECTED / safe wording); anything else is a `[VERIFY]` lead, never voiced until stage R confirms it.

## 4. Voice and persona

**The night-shift engineer who has seen everything**: calm (never sells), dry (the joke is the gap between words and picture), exact (plain words, real numbers on chips, no hedges), on your side (talks to "you"), respectful (switches register instantly when real people were hurt: "Everyone aboard died." + 1.5 s silence).

Jokes (deadpan, the picture carries it): understatement on an absurd fact ("That helped. A little." over 600 t of lead on a tower); literal word -> literal picture ("arm-wrestling a few parked cars" -> cars stacked behind the door); rule of three with a snap ("Don't panic. Don't yank. Wiggle."); a personified part with a job ("The spring starts winning."); a wordless reaction beat (1-2.5 s). **Never**: a joke about a real person's death/injury, a pun on a disaster ("sticky situation" for molasses is a firing offence), a joke the narrator laughs at.

Grammar: **present tense for scenes** (even history: "Ohio, 1974. A cashier slides it across the glass."); second person for scenarios, history has no "you" until the bridge to today; name a real person ONCE, inside a clause, never as a preamble (name/year/place go on a chip); one idea per sentence (two "and"s = two sentences); concrete nouns; verbs that move (shoves, gulps, snaps, swallows, wins, lets go) not "is located / consists of".

Never say: "Let's dive in/look inside/slow time down", "In this video", "Today we", "Have you ever wondered", "Did you know", fascinating/incredible/amazing/mind-blowing/genius/crazy/funny, basically/essentially/simply put/interestingly/surprisingly, "This is called a..." (put the name on a chip, say what it does), manual words (component, mechanism, system as filler, located, utilise, in order to), "The full video is linked below"/subscribe (standalone Shorts), invented words for real people, "we/our channel/I".

## 5. Hook lab

**Rule:** line 1 is the **outcome stated as a contradiction, <= 14 words, over the most extreme image of the story**; the main claim lands within 2.5 s (gate W2). Never as a hook: a question, "When you...", "You'd think..." alone, a definition, a bare date, a number with no contradiction.

| # | Formula | Shape | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| HF1 | Outcome before cause | "This X did Y. [opposite]." | "This glue barely sticks. That's why it's on every desk." |
| HF2 | Object's paradox, "on purpose" | "This X is [wrong-seeming]. On purpose." | "This hydrant is empty. On purpose." |
| HF3 | Hidden agent | "That isn't X doing it. It's Y." | "The hiss a parked truck makes isn't the brakes letting go. It's them grabbing." |
| HF4 | Myth slap | "[Source] lied. [True outcome]." | "Movies lied. Quicksand can't pull you under." |
| HF5 | Conditional stake | "If X happens to you..." | "If this truck's air line bursts, its brakes come on." |
| HF6 | Tiny cause, huge effect | "[Small] -> [world-sized]" | "A soldering iron touched a needle. Now printers boil ink." (superseded by safe wording "this printer") |
| HF7 | Named-after / origin twist | "This X exists because of..." | "This little flap is named after a hijacker who was never caught." |
| HF8 | Time paradox | "It took [absurd time] to do [instant thing]." | "This pack of gum took twenty-five years to scan." |
| HF9 | Second-person dare | "Even [strongest you] can't..." | "Even the strongest person alive can't open this door up here." |

**Hook scorecard (10 points; >= 8 to use; #9 is mandatory = reject whatever the total if 0):** 1 <= 14 words (<= 9 better) · 2 outcome first · 3 contradiction ("wait, what?") · 4 names a frame-0 noun ("this hydrant") · 5 stake (body, money, safety, time, "you") · 6 curiosity gap (mechanism can't be guessed) · 7 plain words a 12-year-old knows · 8 <= 1 number and the number IS the paradox · **9 TRUTH: true in VERIFIED-FACTS wording** · 10 callback-able (holds a word the last line can flip).

**"3 hooks, pick 1" ritual:** (1) write three hooks from three different formulas; (2) score each; (3) read aloud at register speed and time it (contradiction by 2.5 s); (4) check frame 0 can SHOW the hook with the sound off; (5) pick one and record why the others lost (a losing hook is often the best title or thumbnail); (6) plant the callback: underline the hook word the last line will flip (gates W7/W8 check it).

## 6. Rhythm

| Measure | Target | Gate |
|---|---|---|
| Mean sentence length | 7-13 words (sweet 8-10) | W4 |
| Fragments (<= 5 words) | >= 20 % (sweet 25-40 %); too many is also a failure (mean < 7) | W4 |
| Sentences > 15 words | never 3 in a row; none > 28 | W4 |
| Pivot (But/So/Then/Now/Yet/Until/Instead...) | at least every 25 s | W5 |
| Pause before a hard turn | 0.4-1.0 s, **filled with an SFX hit**; silence only in tragedy | W5, P5 |
| Pauses overall | >= 1 per 25 s of runtime | W5 |
| Numbers | <= 1 per sentence | W10 |
| Short | 90-130 words (~35-50 s with pauses) | LEN |
| Long | 750-1,100 words in chained segments | LEN |

Speaking rate by register (words / voiced time): what-if 195-225 (checker 210) · history/explainer/mech-in-place 170-195 (185) · crime/documentary 165-180 (175) · tragedy 155-175 (165); within a video lists speed up and the last line is the slowest (then a 1-2.5 s hold).

Five sentence patterns: (1) **setup then punch** (9-13 words, then <= 5); (2) **pivot sandwich** (setup, `[pause 0.5 . SFX]`, "But...", consequence); (3) **staccato ladder** (one item per 0.8-1.2 s, last held 1.5x: "1838: worse. 1934: worse. 1995: the tower flinched."); (4) **the command** ("Now park it.": works as pivot and cues the shot change); (5) **the one question** (<= 1 per Short, answered in the next line: "The customer? He worked there.").

Markup: `@register what-if|history|crime|tragedy`, `Line. [pause 0.5 . SFX hit]` (the pause AFTER the line = before the next), `[beat]` = 0.5 s, `[silence 1.5]` tragedy only, `## Segment title` per Short-sized segment in longs; chips/gags/shots stay in the shot list, not in the script block; JSON: the pause goes in `pause_after` of the previous sentence. Written vs spoken: put the stake first ("The only thing holding that cap shut is a tiny glass bulb."), numbers on chips, deadpan lists ("A pin, a pin, and a spring.").

## 7. Story recipes (mix 4-6 per Short)

- **R1 located stake**: the first noun after the hook is a place and a person, not a part; dressed fg/mg/bg set; the stake visible. Failure: a place named only on a chip over a void.
- **R2 the ghost**: "You'd think..." in line 2 or 3 (never 1), then a pause; the expectation HAPPENS as a translucent sepia ghost, fails with a red X chip, rewinds (STUDY W3: voiced within 8 s).
- **R3 x-ray in the same shot**: one transition line that is a command/location ("Under the glass...", "Look down the spout..."); dissolve the shell in the world shot with the background still lit (A10); never cut to a grey studio.
- **R4 sabotage**: remove a part in one sentence and show the disaster, on the avatar or a crash dummy only; cut on the impact (H6).
- **R5 ladder of jobs/attempts** (3+ rungs, each <= 2 sentences and <= 1 shot, escalating).
- **R6 personified part** with a desire and a verb ("The spring wants out."); job-badge chip, a physically plausible flinch; never a cartoon face.
- **R7 callback + reframe**: the hook plants a word, the last line flips it; the closing shot rhymes with frame 0.
- **R8 origin flash**: one beat (3-6 s, one shot) of history with a named person, year chip and period set inside a mechanism Short.
- **R9 scale translation**: every big number becomes a body/object on screen ("several tonnes" -> a few parked cars; "60 psi" -> a tug-of-war bar; microseconds -> a frozen coffee drip).

## 8. Formats (Short ~50 s / long 5-7 min; longs are chained Short-sized segments)

**Study templates (STUDY section 8):**
- **T1 What if you...** (0-3 cold open in the extreme situation, hook states the stake · 3-9 naive expectation as ghost · 9-22 mechanism reveal, x-ray in the same shot, gag #1 · 22-34 real proof with date/person/citation chips + reaction insert (gag #2) · 34-44 limit/twist (when it doesn't work) · 44-50 punch + 1-2.5 s hold). Long: cold open + promise 0:00-0:25; rounds 1-3 escalating (everyday -> vehicle/building -> absurd) ~80 s each (expectation, x-ray, gag, twist); real case study 4:25-5:30; callback to round 1 + reframe 5:30-6:00.
- **T2 Crime uncovered** (0-3 impossible clue (macro of the tiny thing) · 3-9 place + date (map fly-in + chip; victims as backs/silhouettes) · 9-20 crime + cover, 3-5 quick shots 1-2 s, never graphic · 20-38 clue chain, 3 clues with chips, the decisive clue shown as a MECHANISM (our strength) · 38-48 reveal, staccato 1 s beats, 0.6-1 s silence before the name · 48-55 legacy "That's why your X now has Y"). Long: cold open 0:00-0:30; Act I world 0:30-1:30; Act II crime + dead ends (2 false leads, time-pressure chip) 1:30-3:00; Act III breakthrough with a 60-90 s forensic mechanism segment 3:00-4:45; Act IV trial/aftermath 4:45-6:00; coda 6:00-6:20.
- **T3 History -> modern object (fix ladder)** (0-3 paradox, outcome before cause · 3-12 original problem in a located set + year chip · 12-20 attempt 1 fails (X chip, gag) · 20-28 attempt 2 partial · 28-38 accident/insight, named person + reaction insert + mechanism x-ray · 38-44 rapid refinement list, 1 s per rung · 44-50 bridge to today "Every X you've ever seen..." + hero shot, sting or hard silence). Long: cold open 0:00-0:25 then 5-7 rungs of 40-60 s each (person, place, date, one failure/gag), rung 4 has the "museum lineup" summary shot, 5:00-6:30 deep cutaway of the modern object as the reward, then the reframe.
- **T4 Character-driven explainer** (everyday moment + worry question · hidden system wakes (x-ray from the same shot) · three layers of protection, 8-9 s each with a "what if it failed" mini-gag · the paranoid last resort, personified · back in the kitchen, reframe). Long: the avatar's day in 5 situations of 50-60 s, finale names the shared principle.
- **T5 Disaster -> design (respectful)** (0-4 the object's odd property · 4-10 place/date, abstracted · 10-30 the event: abstract crowd, force arrows, floor plan, no victims shown · 30-34 the toll as a typographic number + **1.0-2.0 s near-silence** · 34-46 the forgotten fix: patent drawing + inventor chip · 46-55 the rule changed: the modern object performs the fix + 3-6-word reframe). Long: cold open -> event reconstructed 90 s -> investigation 90 s -> the engineering fix in full 3D mechanism 120 s -> where you see it today 60 s. No gags in the event acts; light irony only in the "today" act.

**WRITING.md formats (new):**
- **T6 Mechanism Mystery** (object = suspect): 0-3 the clue (a paradox the viewer has seen) · 3-9 suspect 1 = the obvious explanation as a ghost, X · 9-14 twist witness (origin flash or real proof) · 14-32 the culprit: x-ray in the same shot, 2-3 rungs of what each part does · 32-42 the motive (why designed so: fail-safe/trade-off) + one sabotage gag · 42-50 case closed: clue replayed with its new meaning, <= 10-word reframe. Used: hydrant, barcode, truck brakes, Teflon. Long: 6 "cases" of ~55 s, cold open poses the big clue, case 6 solves it, closing line reframes the opening.
- **T7 Sabotage Test** (prove every part by removing it): 0-3 working object + outcome hook · 3-8 "Take away part 1" failure on the avatar/dummy, X · 8-16 x-ray what part 1 does · 16-24 "Now take away part 2" bigger failure · 24-34 x-ray part 2 + how 1+2 work together · 34-42 "Put them all back": relief slump · 42-50 reframe: the boring object is a stack of tiny refusals. Used: child-resistant cap proposal (Nov 3).
- **T8 Day in the Life** (a part's shift or the avatar's day): hook states the job's absurd stake · 3-12 clocking in (part at rest, job-badge chip) · 12-30 the moment in slow motion with a timer chip · 30-40 the rest of the day (1,000 more times, or never) · 40-50 clocking out, reframe ("It works for one second a year. That second is the job."). Ready for: seatbelt, crosswalk button, fridge.
- **T9 Wrong Gear** (avatar came ready for the imagined danger; the real system handles a different one; matches the covers): 0-3 avatar in the gear in the place + hook (myth wrong) · 3-9 gear's logic = naive expectation (ghost) · 9-24 real physics, x-ray, one literal-metaphor gag (H3) showing the gear is useless · 24-36 real proof with chips (respect rules) · 36-44 the real danger/limit (twist) straight · 44-50 punch: rule of three, avatar ditches/repurposes the gear, reframe. Used: plane door, quicksand, lightning, glow stick.
- **T10 Last Line of Defence** (layers fail until the sacrifice): outcome "This [part] would rather break than let you..." · 3-12 layer 1 works (x-ray) · 12-22 sabotage layer 1 -> layer 2 catches · 22-32 sabotage layer 2 -> layer 3 · 32-42 the sacrifice: last layer breaks itself, slow-mo, hero light goes dark · 42-50 back to normal, reframe personifies the sacrifice. Ready for: microwave interlock, fuse, crumple zone.
- Slate mapping: T6 hydrant/barcode/truck brakes/Teflon · T9/T1 plane door/quicksand/lightning/glow stick · T3 Post-it/faucet/windshield/Pisa/inkjet · T2 Cooper vane/Hitler diaries · T7 cap proposal; longs T3 (helicopter, container), T5 (Comet, molasses), T2 (Crippen).

**How a long is built (chained Shorts with a payoff spine):** cold open 0:00-0:30 = the most extreme image + the hook + **the spine question** only the ending answers · 5-7 segments of 45-70 s, each a Short in miniature (mini-hook line, a turn by 25 s, one gag or one silence by register, a **button line** <= 10 words and quotable) · each segment answers part of the spine and re-asks the rest · a **mechanism reward** segment of 90-150 s near the end (our full-detail cutaway) · coda = callback to the cold-open image + reframe + 2 s hold. No outro, no logo. Longs are mostly environments, machines, maps and diagram layer (people as silhouettes/backs/hands/crowds = C1); the C3 crime long is last on purpose.

## 10. Reframes and callbacks

Last line <= 10 words, reuses a hook word and inverts it. Ten patterns: **P1 word flip** ("Nothing rips. Everything folds."; "The brakes never let go. The air did.") · **P2 job redefinition** ("It stuck around because it barely sticks.") · **P3 agent swap** ("The tyres didn't save you. The metal around you did.") · **P4 every-X bridge** ("Every dot on this page was boiled into place.") · **P5 contents swap** ("The tank was never empty. It was full of frying pan.") · **P6 grip vs pull** ("It can't pull you under. It just won't let go.") · **P7 caught the wrong thing** ("They never caught him. They caught his exit.") · **P8 saved by the threat** ("The engineers saved it by letting it sink.") · **P9 paused, not dead** ("It doesn't die in the freezer. It waits for tomorrow.") · **P10 the plain verdict** (tragedy only: "Six inches of water. That was the whole test.").
Callbacks: write the **plant list** in the slate file before the script (every object/word/person in the first 30 % returns in the last 25 %, W7); spoken callback at most once per Short ("Remember the hiss?" then flip it); visual callback = last shot reprises frame 0's angle with the new meaning; sound callback = the hook's SFX returns as the last hit, slightly slower; longs bring the cold-open image back at the midpoint, half-explained.

## 11. Titles and thumbnail text

- **Title (W1):** a paradox in 5-9 words, object or "you" + contradiction, a pronoun when possible ("This", "Your", "You"); no colon, no "How does...", no number lists, no `?` except a genuine what-if ("What If You Froze a Glow Stick?"). Good: "This Gum Took 25 Years to Scan", "This Flap Is Named After a Hijacker". Retire: "How Does a Soda Can Open Without Losing Its Tab?". The searchable word (quicksand, hydrant, barcode) must be in the title, never only in the thumbnail.
- **Thumbnail text:** **2-4 words, ALL CAPS, Montserrat Black, ending in "?", cream/white with ONE yellow #FFD21F key word** (the only place colour touches text), on a dark real-looking render of the payoff state; thumbnail image = frame 0 (or a frame within the first 3 s).
- **Two recorded rules conflict and both stand:** the user's 2026-10-05 decision says the thumbnail text is a SHORTENED 2-4 word version of the title's question ("How Does a Key Open a Lock?" -> "HOW KEYS WORK?", never a random detail; gate `validate_thumbnail_question` in the 2D era), while WRITING 7.2 says it is the question the title's claim raises and "never repeats the title" ("This Gum Took 25 Years to Scan" -> "**25 YEARS** TO SCAN?"; "Quicksand Can't Swallow You. It's Worse." -> "**STUCK**, NOT SUNK?"). Barcode carries a primary plus an A/B variant (`thumbnail_text_ab`) and the owner picks. Do not decide silently: offer both.
- **Relationship:** the thumbnail asks, the title claims, the video proves; all three never say the same thing; the video must show everything the title claims in the first half and pay it off; no promises the footage can't keep ("explodes" if nothing explodes).

## 12. Table-read checklist (stage T; a fresh agent scores it; >= 90 % with no hard fail) and red flags

Read aloud at register speed with a stopwatch, captions off, imagining the picture. **Hard fails (H):** (1) hook contradiction by 2.5 s and frame 0 shows it sound-off · (2) `script_gates.py` exits 0 · (3) every claim maps to a VERIFIED/CORRECTED/safe-wording row, no `[VERIFY]` line voiced · (4) within 6 s you know WHERE and WHO ("you") · (6) the mechanism reveal is inside the world shot (x-ray, not a cut to a void) · (10) last line quotable and flips a hook word · (11) real people: no invented speech, named once, chips carry names/years · (12) respect register: 1-2 s silence after every casualty line, no gags near · (13) the avatar is faceless, its cover is justified by the beat, tell budget <= 1. Soft: (5) naive expectation voiced and shown · (7) >= 1 gag per 12 s (what-if) / 25 s (history), 0 in harm beats · (8) >= 1 literal metaphor or number drawn (H3) · (9) a ladder of >= 3 rungs · (14) title, thumbnail, frame 0 obey section 11.

**Red flags** ("object in a void": rewrite before shooting a frame if **3 or more** are true): could be narrated over grey · every sentence's subject is a part · no person/place/year in the first 10 s · opens "When you..."/"You'd think..." · no "But" in the first 15 s · > 3 part names in any 10 s · a number with no comparison · manual words · last line is a summary · nothing funny and nobody harmed (gag slots skipped) · no line a friend would quote · ends with a CTA · nothing could go wrong (no stake/failure/ghost) · explains before it shows ("This is a dry-barrel hydrant. It works by...").

## 13. Study rule IDs (STUDY.md section 7; the gates and logs cite these)

**Writing:** W1 paradox title 5-9 words · W2 outcome-first line 1, claim <= 2.5 s, first word <= 0.3 s, most extreme image on frame 0, <= 14 words · W3 naive expectation voiced within 8 s then "But" · W4 rhythm (mean 7-13, max 28, >= 20 % <= 5 words, no 3 long in a row) · W5 pivot at least every 25 s, 0.4-1.0 s pause before each major turn · W6 at least one 3-rung escalation ladder (each rung <= 2 sentences, <= 1 shot) · W7 first-30 % things reused in the last 25 %, >= 1 explicit callback · W8 reframe ending <= 10 words, inverts the hook, hold 1-2.5 s (<= 4.5 s if visual), no CTA/outro/logo · W9 history ends with the modern object on screen + an "every X you've ever seen..." line in the last 15 % · W10 <= 1 number per sentence, scale comparison on screen for anything above everyday scale · W11 names/dates on chips, each spoken at most once · W12 comedy only when no real person is harmed; deaths/injuries: no gags, abstraction/silhouettes, 1.0-2.0 s near-silence after the casualty line, 155-175 WPM · W13 honesty block in the description · W14 accuracy: triple verification + REFERENCE.md rules; never invent dialogue for real people.
**Pacing:** P1 shot length (Shorts: median 2.5-4.5 s, story sections 1.0-2.5 s, reveal/summary holds <= 9 s; every shot > 4 s has a state change at least every 2 s) · P2 cut/highlight within +-0.25 s of the keyword · P3 punch fragments (<= 5 words) get their own shot or hard state change of 0.8-1.8 s · P4 lists: one item per 0.8-1.2 s, a tick/whoosh each, last held 1.5x · P5 pre-turn pause 0.4-1.0 s filled by an SFX hit peaking -3..+3 dB vs speech mean (or deliberate silence, tragedy only), never an unfilled gap without picture motion · P6 register WPM (section 6) · P7 visual energy (share of 0.25 s steps with mean diff > 25): what-if >= 30 %, history >= 20 %, tragedy >= 12 %, static (< 3) <= 12 % outside holds · P8 end hold 1-2.5 s, last frame = hero object or reaction.
**Humour:** H1 gag density (what-if >= 1/12 s, history >= 1/25 s, tragedy 0) · H2 deadpan narrator · H3 literalise >= 1 metaphor or number per Short · H4 reaction insert 1-2.5 s after a reversal · H5 rule of three with snap · H6 slapstick only on the avatar/crash dummy, impacts cut away on the impact frame.
**Animation/characters:** A1 never static (eased camera motion, handheld drift <= 0.3 deg; static frames only in planned reaction holds <= 2.5 s) · A2 located sets (fg/mg/bg, >= 5 props, motivated lighting; studio voids only for the diagram register and <= 30 % of runtime) · A3 environmental life (>= 1 secondary motion source per shot) · A4 idle life (breathing 0.5-1 deg, blink every 2-6 s, weight shift every 4-8 s, no T/A-pose, no pops) · A5 anticipation >= 4 frames + overshoot/settle, cloth/props follow through · A6 contacts (feet slide <= 1 cm, hands touch what they hold, no interpenetration) · A7 faces (>= 4 expressions + eye darts; **overridden for the "you" avatar by the faceless rule**; prefer backs/silhouettes/costumes/hands for harm) · A8 one fixed "you" model and outfit · A9 crowds (>= 3 cycles with random offsets, >= 2 variants per 10 agents, none closer than mid-shot unless hero) · A10 cutaways in the world shot before any void · A11 annotation kit (year/name chip, status chip, call-out, HUD stat card, citation chip, timer chip, map pin: one font family, in/out <= 0.3 s, <= 2 on screen) · A12 at least one designed transition per Short (match cut, map fly-in, x-ray dissolve, whip with motion blur) · A13 sensitive content: crash dummies, abstract crowds, silhouettes, empty rooms; no gore, no realistic real victims · A14 caption keyword colours: **overridden by the user: captions stay white with no coloured word**; red/green are chips.

1 one-line hook · 2 a 60-120-word factual paragraph · 3 an **honesty line** ("3D scenes are illustrations...", "Dramatised and compressed...", "a 3D what-if, not something to try", "X and Y are CG characters, not portraits"; barcode's: dramatised reconstruction, scanner cutaway illustrates the principle slowed down and is not a blueprint, fictional code number, symbolic closing shot, sounds dramatised) · 4 **sources with exact citations** (patent numbers, standards, papers, page/section) · 5 asset licences (music credit text exactly as in `examples/music-library.json`; Poly Haven / ambientCG courtesy; CC-BY items such as Rain/Snow mandatory) then 3-5 hashtags (`#shorts` on Shorts). ## 15. Research and fact discipline

- **Stage R** writes `REFERENCE.md`: (1) **claims table** for every voiced line, chip and picture claim: claim, source URL, date opened, <= 15-word quote, confidence H/M/L (script facts must be H or M; L cut or "the story goes"), (2) "Facts that are NOT claimed and why" (retired contested claims), (3) visual reference with real dimensions (patent figures, manufacturer drawings; estimates tagged [E] or [3D]), (4) script changes per verification round (each round names what changed and why), (5) description material, (6) sources opened with problems. Plan-card source tags: **[C]** opened and checked, **[L]** a lead that has NOT been opened (must be opened and confirmed or cut at stage R). "No fact on a card is a script line until stage R marks it verified."
- **Stage V / verification rounds:** a **fresh-context agent** re-opens every URL and re-checks every number, name, date (0 mismatches). Any wording change after a check needs a fresh re-check of the changed lines **before voicing** (owner rule 8; `voice_o1.py` refuses while the flag is PENDING; barcode logged 44/44 then 19/19 quotes found word for word on freshly fetched sources). "Done" in a log is unverified until a render shows it. Independent reviewers (not the author) score the table read and the frame-0 look.
- **Examples of discipline (PLAN-v2 hooks changed because false or over-reaching):** barcode gum is NOT in a museum (the real pack stayed with Clyde Dawson); truck "stops harder with no air" unsupported; Post-it "a failure / super-glue" is the AVOID myth (use "barely sticks"); plane door "the sky leans on it" is wrong physics (it is cabin air pressure); faucet "nobody gets burned" false; the dropped-flask windshield legend is told as "the story goes"; Hitler-diaries "detergent" link unverified (the hook is the paper); "your printer boils ink" false for piezo printers (say "this printer"); glow-stick "lasts all night" unverified (qualitative cold vs warm only); lightning "windows up / don't touch" advice unverified; container "everything you own" over-reaches; molasses "changed engineering law" unverified. An unqualified "first scan" was false as worded (a 1972 Kroger trial existed), so it became "the modern barcode's first live scan".
- **Facts-table status labels in slate files:** VERIFIED/CORRECTED (as in VERIFIED-FACTS, voiced) · SAFE (safe wording verbatim or tighter) · ESTIMATE (labelled as estimate: "several", "a few") · DERIVED (arithmetic/calendar computed from verified data) · PLAN [C] (checked 2026-10-10: mechanism wording only) · PHYSICS (school-level physics stated generally with no numbers; stage R attaches a textbook citation; never a historical/numeric claim) · **[VERIFY]** = a lead, not voiced, shown in "pending inserts" only.
- **Topic exclusion before adding any topic:** grep `examples/`, `production-batches/*/topics/`, `release-calendar/`, `production-3d/`, `experiments/zdf-3d*/` and the ledger; no close variants (U-bend/sink trap, microwave, plane vacuum toilet, panic bar, elevator brakes without the user's OK).
- Sensitive-register rules for real events (Asiana 8124, Alaska 1282, Comet, molasses, Crippen, Cooper): no real passengers/crew/victims shown, exteriors + diagram only, no gags near harm, no swastikas/Nazi imagery beyond plain black notebooks (also a platform-policy risk), "CG characters, not portraits".

## 16. Workflow: topic card -> locked script

1. Facts first: copy the topic's VERIFIED-FACTS block into the slate facts table, strike every UNVERIFIED item, write the plant list. 2. 3 hooks, pick 1 (section 5). 3. Pick the template (T1-T10) and paste its beat table. 4. Draft at 110-125 words in the markup. 5. `python production-3d/tools/script_gates.py writing/slate/<file>.md --timeline` until exit 0. 6. Shot list: one shot per beat (15-25 per Short), gag slots, located set, the x-ray moment, chips, SFX in pauses. 7. Avatar pass (cover, beat justification, glyph, tell) against `production-3d-characters.md`. 8. Table read by a fresh agent (section 12). 9. Port to `script.short.json` (each pause marker -> `pause_after` on the previous line, `register` set) and re-run the gates on the JSON.
