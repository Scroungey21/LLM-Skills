# Editorial and research standards (news-explainer)

Sources merged: old orchestrator skill `editorial-standards`, `VIDEO_ORCHESTRATION_PLAYBOOK` (editorial parts), master-spec section 2
(constraints), `CREATOR_STYLE_GUIDE` section 5 (sourcing). Voice and visual-choice rules live in `creator-style-guide.md`.

## Editorial promise

Each video leaves a curious viewer understanding **what happened, how it works, why it matters, what is confirmed, and what
remains uncertain**. Conversational, not a corporate briefing. Accuracy and explanation outrank speed to publish. The aim is
*publishable editorial quality* - accurate reporting, original analysis, natural delivery, useful visuals, clear sourcing and honest
compliance with YouTube's disclosure rules - **not** disguising authorship or evading platform systems (never promise or claim that).

## Hard constraints (encode in any script/review prompt, not as an afterthought)

- **No fabrication**: no invented quote, statistic, attribution, product, model, release, benchmark, vulnerability, image or official
  position. Enforced by a grounding pass (every claim checked against the stored source); untraceable claims are **flagged for the human
  at Gate 1, never silently deleted**, and a blocking flag must be resolved before audio.
- **Cyber guardrail**: explain what happened, the mechanism *conceptually*, impact, timeline, mitigation/patch status. Never functional
  exploit code, working PoC payloads, or step-by-step weaponisation. High-level mechanism diagrams and defensive explanation are fine.
- **Sources retained**: every script carries its source URL(s); they feed the description, `meta.json.sources`, and fact-check ground truth.
- **Third-party media**: the master spec says all visuals are generated by Remotion components (no copyrighted third-party media); the
  playbook ranks licensed/fair-use source evidence (product UI, terminal output, chart, advisory excerpt) first. **Both are on record.**
  Working rule: source evidence only as faithful, attributed, fair-use-assessed excerpts or purpose-built `SourceCitation`/diagram
  components; never fabricate a product screen or pass illustrative imagery off as evidence. Real-media ingestion (verified download,
  rights annotation, crop, timed source clip) is **not built** - a production must distinguish actual sourced captures from procedural
  diagrams and must not claim the visual-evidence gate is complete without them.

## Research protocol

1. Define the viewer promise in one sentence (what changed, why it matters, what the viewer will understand). Establish the precise claim and a **date boundary**; record the publication cutoff time (stored as `fact_check.cutoff` in `meta.json`).
2. Browse current information for every news topic. Prefer primary sources: vendor announcements/model/system cards, government or
   standards-body advisories, CVE records, original papers, filings, repositories, direct technical documentation.
3. Secondary reporting only for context, independent verification, or documented criticism - never replacing an available primary source.
4. Extract discrete claims, numbers, dates, limitations, quotations; record the supporting URL (and access time for any planned screenshot/clip).
5. Classify each in the claim ledger as **verified fact / attributed (vendor or reported) claim / inference / estimate / opinion**; never narrate one as another.
6. Qualify benchmarks, battery figures, prices, affected versions, exploit scope, availability, geography, test conditions. Prices, availability,
   benchmarks, vulnerabilities, company statements and legal/policy status are *volatile*.
7. Recheck URLs and unstable facts immediately before audio generation and again before final metadata. Unverifiable -> attribute precisely, qualify, or remove.
8. Put source URLs in both descriptions and the `sources` array (objects with `title`, `url`, `type` e.g. `primary`).
9. **Independent-source rule (recommended, currently unmet):** cite at least one independent/secondary source (analysis, benchmark, corroborating
   report) next to the primary, and list all in the description. In the 2026-09 audit 14 of 15 productions had exactly 1 source (one had 2) while the
   benchmark channel cites 5-11. This is a pipeline default to fix, not yet enforced - do it by hand now.

## Definition of done (editorial side)

A production is complete only with: a claim->source ledger; a conversational script whose facts survive a final time-sensitive recheck; a visual beat
for every narration beat (no generic slide stays on screen just because narration continues); legible 1440p typography + kinetic captions;
a Short that stands alone, keeps the central nuance and ends with the exact CTA; clean automated tests plus human review of the opening, a
middle mechanism section, every high-risk claim, and the closing.

## Long-form structure

Order: hook -> essential context -> mechanism -> evidence -> limitation/counterpoint -> consequence -> takeaway.

- **Hook in the first 15-20 s**: a concrete scenario, surprising implication, or precise question; open with the consequence/tension, never a greeting or a headline summary. (Stronger hook tier: `creator-style-guide.md`.)
- Mechanism in plain language with concrete nouns that map to visuals. Introduce a term only when useful and explain it at first use.
- Reveal the key caveat/threat/limitation/twist *after* the viewer understands the mechanism.
- Anchor significance with sourced numbers and relatable comparisons; keep exact values on screen long enough to read. Translate each key number into a comparison or consequence.
- End on a clear, useful takeaway, not a repeated summary.
- Vary sentence length and paragraph rhythm. Avoid military-briefing openers, fake suspense, filler, repetitive conclusions, breathless hype, vague claims ("changes everything"). Use contractions, fragments, real transitions; **never add fake stumbles or verbal filler**.

### Beat and pacing numbers

- A beat = one visual idea. Code constants (`narration_beats.py`): `TARGET_BEAT_WORDS=26`, `MAX_BEAT_WORDS=34`, `NARRATION_WORDS_PER_SECOND=3.0`,
  beat duration clamped 3,000-12,000 ms. Playbook wording: 18-30 words, <=34, normally 6-12 s. Split mechanism, evidence, numbers and consequences into separate beats.
- Change/reveal/redirect the visual within each 6-12 s beat; a longer section needs internal sub-beats, not a static card.
- Script runtime target (`scriptwriter.py`): source length <=2,000 chars -> 240 s, <=6,000 -> 420 s, larger capped at `_MAX_RUNTIME_SECONDS=600`; `WORDS_PER_MINUTE=225` (calibrated when the tempo was 1.5 - see `audio-tts.md`; the playbook says write for 175-190 wpm but prioritise intelligibility); `HOOK_RESERVE_FRACTION=0.10`. Depth scales with source richness: do not pad a thin post, do not truncate a full advisory. Increase information density rather than shortening finished videos.
- Read every paragraph aloud; rewrite stacked clauses, repeated sentence openings, abstract noun chains and phrases the voice stresses badly.
- Pattern-interrupt cadence per archetype: deep-dive 45 s, weekly-rundown 20 s, retrospective 60 s, myth-debunking 45 s (see `pipeline-and-architecture.md`).
- Relatable contrast (channel-wide directive): after an impressive spec/scale figure, follow immediately with a mundane concrete comparison from everyday life - make the abstraction felt, never mock the achievement.
- Phonetic TTS text vs display text: TTS needs spelled-out numbers/versions/CVEs ("V eight", "CVE twenty twenty-six ..."), but that same string leaks onto captions/cards. Author **two parallel fields** per numeric mention (spoken form, display form); flag any card whose primary value is a spelled-out string. No safe automatic reverse conversion exists.

## Cybersecurity standard (deeper than ordinary news recaps)

Explain: what the affected system is and what it controls; the attacker's prerequisite access and realistic path; the vulnerability/technique in non-specialist
language; the difference between **capability, observed exploitation, proof of concept, and speculation**; impact boundaries and what the advisory does *not*
establish; practical defensive guidance *from the source*; why it matters to ordinary people without sensationalising. Long-form is intentionally granular (the
reviewed PLC video is 33 scenes / 1,364 words) and explains relevant systems, threats and terminology organically for non-technical or short-attention viewers.
Do not trivialise victims; a title/thumbnail must not imply a conclusion the evidence does not support (e.g. "AI attacked a factory" vs "AI-generated scripts in a campaign").

## AI/ML standard

Separate benchmark marketing from independent real-world performance. State modalities, input/output limits, pricing, availability and model-card caveats precisely.
Explain benchmark names only when needed; never treat one score as general intelligence. Label vendor-provided numbers as **vendor claims** unless independently confirmed.

## Consumer / classic-technology standard

State exact model/variant, regional pricing, availability, compatibility and test assumptions. Explain practical benefit and trade-off rather than reciting a spec
table. For battery life, durability, emergency services or health features preserve the manufacturer's conditions and disclaimers.

## Policy / corporate / antitrust stories

(Derived from the shipped Amazon/FTC, Google AdX and EU AI Act productions, not from a separate rule doc.) Same rules: primary document first (court order, complaint, filing, regulation text), attribute allegations as allegations ("alleged"), keep scope precise
(what the ruling ordered vs what was requested). Use `RedactedDocument`-style framing only for genuinely legal-document stories (component not built, see style guide).

## Short standard

- A standalone compressed explanation, not a random excerpt and not a speed-up of the long script (a rewrite).
- One strong hook, the essential mechanism/news, the most important caveat/proof point + consequence, the takeaway. Brisk; vertical safe areas clear.
- Fixed target ~55 s (`SHORTS_TARGET_RUNTIME_SECONDS=55`; reviewed Shorts ran 25-38 s). Scoped by format, never by source length.
- Narration ends with exactly the CTA constant; the final screen is a `chapter-card` carrying the CTA text (title "FULL VIDEO", subtitle "Link in the description").
- Must show a primary-source citation screen at least once (verified badge + domain + headline), ideally within the first 30-40% of runtime (LITMUS rule 6).
- Short description contains the information-source URLs and, once the long video exists, a `Full Video: <url>` line (uploader fills it).

## Titles, descriptions, tags, thumbnail copy

- **Title**: specific, accurate, curiosity-forward, faithful to the actual script. 5-13 words / ~31-78 chars worked on the benchmark channel; precise numbers as
  credibility anchors; parentheticals to clarify an acronym/entity. Archetypes to rotate: contrarian debunk ("Everyone Got X Wrong"), incident/post-mortem,
  architectural breakdown, paradigm-shift/milestone, concept-to-fix. Contrarian debunk is the one under-used archetype - include in rotation, never force.
  **Grep every generated title/description for stray pipeline artifacts** (the benchmark channel leaked a "(49 chars)" length-check token).
- **Description** (full): concise summary with context/qualifications -> `Primary source(s):` list with direct URLs -> (recommended, unbuilt) YouTube chapter
  timestamps `00:00 - Label` generated from the in-video `ChapterCard` scene boundaries -> hashtags -> alternative titles -> audio note + any required music
  attribution. The long video has no self-referential "watch the full video" line. Descriptions currently carry no subscribe/social boilerplate.
- **Tags**: relevant, non-spammy, combined <=500 characters (trim extras, never the mechanically sourced tags). Baseline extras used on all videos: "tech news explained", "evidence-based tech news", "how it works".
- **Thumbnail copy**: 2-5 words, high contrast, legible at mobile size, truthful. The text should be the *claim/hook* ("GOODBYE VRAM LIMITS"-style), not a topic label
  ("CRITICAL CYBER RISK"); it may heighten curiosity but not overstate evidence. Examples shipped: "AI-WRITTEN ATTACKS", "FLASH GOT SERIOUS", "57 DAYS. NO PHONE.", "RESTART CHROME NOW". Visual rules: `visual-rendering.md`.

## Chapters

Even a ~3-minute video should show 3-4 named on-screen `ChapterCard` labels (not only the opening scene) - they signal a structured investigation and are the source
for description timestamps.
